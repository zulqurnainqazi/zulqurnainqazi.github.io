My research examines how education policy shapes people's opportunities — especially for those who move across borders. It is organized around four strands.

## International students, policy, and the transition to work

My dissertation follows international master's students in STEM-designated programs in the United States as they prepare for the U.S. job market. Using an **aspirations, capabilities, and agency** framework, I study how program timelines, shifting immigration policy, and economic pressures such as housing precarity shape students' employability and post-study transitions. Related work examines student visa policy through social science theory and how university websites frame international student mobility policy and recruitment.

## Education policy analysis: technology and equity

I analyze how education systems govern technology in learning — including an **equity funnel framework** for analyzing ICT-in-education policy and a comparative analysis of mobile phone ban policies in **Ohio, USA, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan**.

## Immigrant youth, identity, and digital storytelling

With collaborators, I use digital storytelling as a research method to explore the mathematical identities of immigrant and transnational youth, including Muslim immigrant girls, as an approach to belonging and epistemic resistance.

## AI, learning, and agency

I study learner agency in AI-mediated learning and the ethical use of AI in education, from early childhood to K-12 language classrooms.

## Methods

Quantitative and qualitative methods, including structural equation modeling, generalized linear modeling, ethnography, narrative inquiry, and comparative case study.
